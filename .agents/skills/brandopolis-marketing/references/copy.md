# Copy and plain-language editing

Adapted from Marketing Skills `copywriting` and `copy-editing` (see [PROVENANCE](PROVENANCE.md)). Spanish (es-MX)
examples; the brand's approved versions are the only source of facts.

## Before writing

1. Which area and piece is it (oferta, publicación, anuncio, página, presentación, instrucciones para diseñador)?
2. One primary action per piece. Name it before writing.
3. Collect from the brand's approved versions: customer and need, offer and result, differences, proof with its
   source, published verbal identity. Anything missing becomes `[falta: …]`, never filler.

## Principles

- **Clear before clever.** If the reader must decode a line, rewrite it.
- **Benefit, then feature.** «Recibe tu café cada mes sin pedirlo» before «suscripción mensual».
- **Specific before vague.** «De 4 horas a 15 minutos» instead of «ahorra tiempo». No number without a source.
- **The customer's words.** Reuse the language of the approved Primary Customer and real evidence, not ours.
- **One idea per section,** in an order that builds the argument.
- **Honest.** No invented statistics, testimonials, scarcity or guarantees.

## Machine-sounding patterns to remove

Clients distrust copy that reads as generated; a single tell makes the reader doubt the claims around it.

1. Contrast reveals: «No es X, es Y», «No solo X, sino Y». Say Y with its reason.
2. Negation lists: «Sin contratos, sin esperas, sin complicaciones». Say what does happen; keep one real absence
   near the action («Sin tarjeta»).
3. Trailing pile-ups after a complete claim. End the sentence at the claim.
4. Self-answered questions and colon reveals: «¿El resultado? …», «Lo mejor: …».
5. Stock openers: «En el mundo actual…», «Lleva tu negocio al siguiente nivel», «Descubre el poder de…».
6. Long dashes in headlines, ads, posts and subject lines.
7. Empty intensifiers and buzzwords (innovador, potente, integral, sin fricciones, optimizar). Replace with the fact.
8. Swap test: if the line works unchanged for a competitor, it lacks the brand's difference. Flag
   `[falta: diferencia]` instead of inventing one.

## Editing in sweeps

Read the draft once per question, fixing only that question each time:

1. **Clarity:** can a newcomer understand every sentence? Words a customer would not say?
2. **Voice:** does it match the published verbal identity and the tú of the product?
3. **So what:** does each feature say what it means for the customer?
4. **Prove it:** is every claim backed by approved evidence with source and version?
5. **Specificity:** numbers, examples and names instead of adjectives?
6. **Feeling:** does it name the real situation of the customer without exaggeration?
7. **Risk:** does it answer the main doubt near the action (price, time, cancellation, privacy)?

Quick checks: sentences over 20 words, passive voice, exclamation marks, «muy», «realmente», jargon, mixed audiences
in one piece, the action buried at the end.

## Output

Deliver the draft plus a short list of what is `[falta: …]` and which approved version each claim used. Never mark a
draft approved; the editorial state belongs to the person.
